**Usage limits no longer strand task children or prematurely skip their provider's other models.** A usage-limit turn settles after the engine's stated retry delay plus one heartbeat interval (10 s by default), unless the retry starts. Ambiguous limits get one same-provider probe, timed from its first provider request. A probe's usage-limit answer is remembered until its own stated reset; a timeout or other failure counts as account-spent for only 30 minutes. Expired resets stay expired, cancelled handoffs leave no probe, and spawn, resume and revival all avoid remembered limits. Spawn refusals identify themselves as `failure_kind: usage_limit`. ## 2026-10-26 - Usage-limit turns settle in bounded time; limit scope is confirmed before a provider is skipped (#11388)

`runners/rpc/turn-settlement.ts`: a held usage-limit error (any `usageLimitScope`) settles at its first confirmation,
one `settleConfirmMs` (the handle's heartbeat interval) after its `agent_end`, regardless of live wake sources. An
`agent_end { willRetry: true }` carrying a usage limit is held the same way, so a retry the engine announces but never
starts still settles. An announced retry gets its stated delay (auto_retry_start.delayMs, the end's delayMs, or
Retry-After) plus the confirmation interval to start. After a usage-limit end, a later terminal usage-limit end (a monitor wake, a steer or the engine's
retry re-hitting the spent limit) settles at once. A non-limit error held by a live wake source now re-arms its
confirmation instead of returning without one.

`manager/credential-failure.ts`: `usageLimitScope` returns `account` only when the text proves it (credits, balance,
billing, a session window, the provider naming the account or organization) and `ambiguous` otherwise. senpi's
"<provider> account usage limit has been reached" rewrite does not count as proof. `runtimeFallbackCandidates` drops
every same-provider rung on a confirmed account limit (they used to move last). An ambiguous limit keeps list order, so
the next same-provider rung is tried once. A previous same-provider attempt proves nothing unless it itself hit a
usage limit or the current rung is an active probe.

`manager/usage-limit-memory.ts`: the task manager remembers spent limits per provider (account) and per provider + model family until the
stated `Resets at <ISO>`, else `LIMIT_MEMORY_DEFAULT_MS` (60 min). `runtimeFallbackCandidates` skips remembered rungs, and a new task
starts on the first rung the memory clears or is refused at spawn with the remembered reason. Any failure of the probe rung confirms the
provider's account limit for routing, but a timeout or non-limit failure is remembered for only
`LIMIT_PROBE_FAILURE_MEMORY_MS` (30 min). Only a usage-limit answer from the probe itself inherits its stated reset;
a reset already in the past expires immediately. Spawn refusals carry `failure_kind: usage_limit`, and cold resume
and warm revival use the same spent-limit routing. `manager/usage-limit-probe.ts`: the probe rung's outcome is bounded
by `limitProbeTimeoutMs` (`LIMIT_PROBE_TIMEOUT_MS`, 60 s), starting at `before_provider_request`, not session creation
or context preparation. Abandoned handoffs leave no probe entry.
