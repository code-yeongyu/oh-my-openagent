## 2026-11-28 - A DAG cancel aborts an in-process child's active turn (#15363)

The DAG drain's stop of an attached child (`abort: "skip"`) no longer defers an in-process child's teardown to its outcome: `cancelTask` always awaits `destroyResidentTask(taskId, "cancel")`, and the lifecycle's `teardownHandle` aborts every in-process handle (bounded, best effort) before disposing it. The deferral (`destroyAfterSettlement`), the `cancel_without_abort` destroy cause and the manager's lane-slot exception for it are removed. The deferral guarded against an engine that floated a retry rejection when an active session was aborted or disposed; the pinned engine does not (abort then dispose of an active stream, of a turn in its retry backoff and of a running tool all end the turn `cancelled` with nothing unhandled), while a dispose alone leaves an active turn running. The skipped abort keeps its other meanings: steering sends no abort of its own, a finished resident is neither released nor counted for undelivered messages, and a recovering connection is cancelled at once. `runners/in-process/cancel-active-turn.integration.test.ts` drives the real engine in a fresh process per case and fails if an engine floats a rejection again.
